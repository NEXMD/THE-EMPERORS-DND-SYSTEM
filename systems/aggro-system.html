AGGRO SYSTEM

THREAT, ATTENTION & ENEMY TARGETING

A shared enemy-targeting system built around each player's starting aggro, combat actions, survival, and the instincts and intelligence of their enemies.

COMPLETE BASELINE Five player-aggro tiers · Shared encounter aggro · DM-controlled enemy behaviour

SYSTEM OVERVIEW

How Aggro Is Organised

Player aggro is calculated in tiers. Enemy behaviour is documented separately.

TIER 0

Base Aggro

Every player begins from the same reference value.

100 AGGRO

TIER 3

All enemies in an encounter use one shared aggro pool. There are no separate aggro tables for individual enemies.

TIER 0 / UNIVERSAL START

Base Aggro

BASE VALUE

Every player begins with 100 Base Aggro.

This is the universal reference before CHA, equipment, Stealth, size, or other encounter-start adjustments are applied.

TIER 1 / INDIVIDUAL START

Player's Aggro

Recalculate whenever a character enters combat, using their current CHA and equipment.

CHARISMA FORMULA

A = 100 - 2 × (Modified CHA - 10)

  • Use the actual CHA ability score, not the CHA modifier.
  • CHA 10 is neutral: 100 aggro before direct item modifiers.
  • Every CHA point above 10 reduces aggro by 2.
  • Every CHA point below 10 increases aggro by 2.
  • Items may increase CHA or directly reduce aggro.
  • Calculate item-based CHA changes first; apply direct aggro item modifiers afterward.

TIER 2 / CONDITIONAL REDUCTION

Player's Aggro (Stealth)

The Stealth modifier comes from the Stealth skill modifier, not the DEX score.

Conditions for Stealth

  • The player must remain unseen and undetected.
  • Attacking, healing, or performing any supporting action removes the Stealth advantage.
  • If an enemy notices or detects the player, the reduction is cancelled.
  • Physical interaction or touching an enemy in a way that reveals the player's presence also cancels the reduction.
  • CHA and Stealth reductions can stack.

When Stealth is lost

Return to normal Player's Aggro first, then add the aggro generated by the action that revealed the player.

EXAMPLE

83 normal Player's Aggro + 20 damage aggro = 103 combat aggro

The rules do not specify a decimal-rounding convention for Stealth results.

TIER 3 / COMBAT ACCUMULATION

Player's Aggro in Combat

Combat actions add aggro to the player's accumulated combat value.

Damage-generated aggro

Character typeConversionFormula40 damage example
Normal character1 damage = 1 aggroDamage × 1+40
Tank1 damage = 2 aggroDamage × 2+80

The Tank multiplier compensates for typically lower damage output. It applies to damage-generated aggro, not healing or HP-loss aggro.

Healing-generated aggro

HEALING CONVERSION

1 effective HP restored = +1 aggro to the healer

  • Only effective HP restored generates aggro.
  • Overhealing generates no aggro.
  • Healing aggro belongs to the healer, not the healed player.

HP-loss aggro

PER DAMAGE EVENT · ROUND DOWN

HP-loss aggro = floor(damage received ÷ 5)

Damage eventCalculationAggro gained
16 damage in Round 3floor(16 ÷ 5)+3
5 damage in Round 4floor(5 ÷ 5)+1
8 damage in one eventfloor(8 ÷ 5)+1
12 damage in one eventfloor(12 ÷ 5)+2

Calculate each separate damage event individually, round down, and add the result to accumulated combat aggro. Do not combine separate hits into one calculation. Poison, burning, and other ongoing damage-over-time effects generate no aggro.

Other aggro additions

  • Taunts may add aggro using the DM reference ranges below.
  • Failure on an Intelligent Support Detection CHA check may add a small DM-determined amount of aggro.

All enemies in the encounter use one shared aggro pool. The system does not maintain separate aggro values for every enemy.

TIER 4 / AGGRO REDUCTION

Aggro Decay

A player who does not attack or heal loses 5 accumulated aggro on their turn.

DECAY

-5 aggro per player turn without attacking or healing

ActionPrevents decay?
AttackYes
HealYes
MovementNo
Defensive actionNo
Utility actionNo
Do nothingNo
  • Decay applies to all accumulated aggro, not only Base Aggro.
  • A defensive or utility action prevents decay only if it also involves attacking or healing.
  • Small attacks can reset decay. This is an intentional tactical trade-off because attacking can endanger teammates.

Unconscious players

  • Players at 0 HP retain their accumulated aggro.
  • Unconscious players cannot generate additional aggro.
  • They lose 5 aggro for every round they remain unconscious.
  • After revival, they retain the remaining aggro after those deductions.
EXAMPLE

180 aggro - (3 unconscious rounds × 5) = 165 aggro on revival

Minimum accumulated combat aggro: 30. Maximum: unlimited.

TIER 5 / ENCOUNTER RESET

End of Combat

POST-COMBAT RULE

Accumulated Combat Aggro → normal Player's Aggro

When combat ends, every player's aggro is reset to their normal Tier 1 Player's Aggro. Combat-generated aggro does not carry into the next encounter.

Instincts influence target preference; they do not override the shared aggro ranking.

10% instinct bonus

  • At the beginning of combat, the DM identifies the player or players who match the enemies' instincts.
  • Each identified player receives +10% of their Player's Aggro.
  • The bonus does not accumulate and is not repeated every round.
  • The bonus changes the aggro value; it does not force the enemy to attack that player.
  • Another player may still have the highest aggro and remain more likely to be targeted.

Mixed-species encounters

When multiple species are present, the DM decides which species' instincts govern the encounter. The same 10% principle applies.

Instincts are behavioural tendencies, not mandatory targeting rules. The DM retains control over enemy behaviour.

ENEMY BEHAVIOUR / 02

Intelligent Support Detection

One check only, at the beginning of combat.

  1. An intelligent enemy identifies a player operating in a rear-guard support role.
  2. The identified player makes a CHA check.
  3. On success, the player receives no additional aggro from this check.
  4. On failure, the player gains a small amount of additional aggro.

TARGETING PRINCIPLES

Shared Aggro & DM Control

  • All enemies in an encounter use the same shared player aggro values.
  • The player with the highest aggro is generally more likely to attract attention, but is not guaranteed to be selected.
  • Enemy instincts, intelligence, personality, and tactical priorities may influence target selection.
  • When players have equal aggro, the DM decides which player is targeted.
  • There is no proximity-based aggro adjustment.
  • There are no separate aggro tables for individual enemies.

No universal target-selection probability formula or weighted spin-wheel procedure is part of the approved baseline.

DM REFERENCE

Adjudication Notes

The core system is complete. These specific edge cases do not have a universal formula in Version 1.0.

  • AoE damage: No special rule determines whether aggro is calculated per enemy hit or from total damage dealt.
  • Mixed-intelligence encounters: Size adjustments apply against Low-INT enemies and are ignored by Average-INT and High-INT enemies. No separate calculation procedure is established for mixed groups using the shared pool.
  • Starting-adjustment order: No universal precedence formula is specified for every combination of Stealth, size, and instinct adjustments.
  • Stealth decimals: A decimal-rounding convention is not specified.
  • INT thresholds: Numerical boundaries are not established; the DM classifies creatures.
  • Target selection: Aggro informs attention and likelihood, but no fixed probability formula is established.

These notes preserve DM discretion. They do not change established rules or make the completed baseline incomplete.
