TIER 0

Base Aggro

UNIVERSAL STARTING VALUE

Every player begins with 100 Base Aggro.

TIER 1

Player's Aggro

Calculate the individual starting value when the character enters combat.

CHARISMA FORMULA

A = 100 - 2 × (Modified CHA - 10)

  • Use the actual Charisma score, not the Charisma modifier.
  • CHA 10 is neutral and gives 100 CHA-adjusted aggro.
  • Every point above CHA 10 reduces aggro by 2.
  • Every point below CHA 10 increases aggro by 2.
  • Equipment that changes CHA changes the score used in the formula.
  • Items may also have direct aggro effects; apply the item's written effect.

Equipment example

CHA 14 with an item that raises CHA by 2 becomes modified CHA 16: 100 - 2 × (16 - 10) = 88 before any direct aggro item effect.

Use the Stealth skill modifier, not the DEX score.

When Stealth applies

  • The player must remain unseen and undetected.
  • Stealth and CHA reductions can stack.
  • Physical interaction or touching an enemy in a way that reveals the player's presence cancels the reduction.
  • If the enemy notices or detects the player, the reduction is cancelled.
  • Attacking, healing, or performing a support action cancels the Stealth advantage.

When Stealth is lost

Restore normal Tier 1 Player's Aggro first. Then add the aggro generated by the action that revealed the player.

EXAMPLE

88 normal Player's Aggro + 20 damage aggro = 108 combat aggro

The recovered rules do not specify a decimal-rounding convention for Stealth calculations.

TIER 3

Player's Aggro in Combat

The full encounter accumulation tier. Damage, healing, HP-loss, and other listed sources contribute to current encounter aggro.

Start from the applicable Player's Aggro value. Add relevant encounter-start modifiers, then accumulate combat-generated aggro throughout the fight.

A. Creature-size modifier — Low-INT enemies only

The size modifier is added at the beginning of combat and only applies against Low-INT enemies. Average-INT and High-INT enemies ignore it.

B. Enemy instinct bonus — +10%

  • At combat start, the DM identifies the player or players matching the relevant enemy instinct.
  • Each DM-selected player receives +10% of their Player's Aggro.
  • The bonus is applied once and does not accumulate or repeat every round.
  • The bonus raises the player's likelihood of being selected but does not force the enemy to attack them.
  • A different player with higher total aggro can still be more likely to be selected.
  • In a mixed-species encounter, the DM decides which species' instinct governs the encounter.

C. Intelligent Support Detection

An intelligent enemy may identify a player operating in a rear-guard support role. This procedure happens once at the beginning of combat only, not every round.

  1. The enemy identifies a rear-guard support player.
  2. The identified player makes a CHA check.
  3. Success: no additional aggro from this check.
  4. Failure: the player gains a small additional amount of aggro.

The DM determines the CHA check DC, the failure aggro amount, and whether the enemy can identify the support player in the situation. Examples include intelligent humanoids, demons, hunters, elves, humanoid tacticians, and other enemies capable of tactical reasoning.

D. Damage-generated aggro

Character typeConversion40 damage example
Normal character1 damage = 1 aggro+40
Tank1 damage = 2 aggro+80

The Tank multiplier compensates for typically lower damage output and helps Tanks maintain enemy attention. It applies to damage-generated aggro only.

E. Healing-generated aggro

HEALING CONVERSION

1 effective HP restored = +1 aggro to the healer

  • Only effective HP actually restored counts.
  • Overhealing generates 0 aggro.
  • The healer receives the healing aggro; the healed player receives 0 from that healing.
  • A healer may become the highest-aggro player if accumulated healing exceeds other players' accumulated aggro.

F. HP-loss aggro

  • Intentionally taking damage to generate aggro is allowed under the current design; it is a tactical trade-off because it endangers the player.

G. Damage-over-time exclusion

Poison damage, burning damage, and other ongoing damage-over-time effects generate no aggro.

H. Taunts and aggro manipulation

Taunt strengthAdditional aggro
Minor+5 to +10

The DM decides whether the enemy responds, which category applies, and the exact amount within the range. A taunt adds aggro; it does not force an enemy to target the taunter.

I. Shared aggro pool

All enemies in one encounter use one shared aggro pool. There are no separate aggro tables for individual enemies. All combat sources are accumulated on the player's encounter aggro value.

J. Target selection — weighted spin wheel

PLAYER'S SPIN-WHEEL SHARE

P = (Player's current aggro ÷ Total party aggro) × 100%

  1. Determine each party member's current aggro.
  2. Add all party members' values to get total party aggro.
  3. Calculate each player's percentage from their share of the total.
  4. Set the spin-wheel weights using those percentages.
  5. Spin the wheel to select the target.

The highest-aggro player has the highest probability, not a guarantee. A healer can become the most likely target if their healing aggro overtakes another player's damage aggro. Aggro changes alter the weights; they do not automatically force a target switch.

The mandatory frequency for re-spinning or reselecting a target is not specified. The DM decides when a new target selection is required.

K. Aggro ties

When multiple players have equal aggro, the DM decides which player the enemy targets.

TIER 4

Aggro Decay

This is a separate tier. Its floor is 0, not the Tier 1 minimum of 30.

NORMAL DECAY

Current accumulated aggro - 5 per turn without attacking or healing

Player actionPrevents the -5 decay?
AttackingYes
HealingYes
MovementNo
Defensive actionNo
Utility actionNo
Doing nothingNo
  • Defensive or utility actions only prevent decay if they also involve attacking or healing.
  • Decay affects all accumulated aggro: Base Aggro, damage, healing, HP-loss, taunt, and other accumulated factors.
  • Aggro cannot fall below 0.
  • Small attacks can reset decay. This is an intentional trade-off because attacking can endanger teammates.

Unconscious players

  • A player at 0 HP retains accumulated aggro.
  • An unconscious player cannot generate additional aggro.
  • An unconscious player loses 5 aggro per round while unconscious.
  • Aggro cannot fall below 0.

Unresolved: whether retained aggro becomes active immediately upon revival, and whether the unconscious -5 per round stacks with ordinary inactive-turn decay for the same interval. These points require a DM ruling; they are not silently decided here.

TIER 5

End of Combat

RESET RULE

Accumulated Combat Aggro → normal Tier 1 Player's Aggro

When combat ends, each player's accumulated combat aggro is cleared. The next encounter begins from normal Player's Aggro, recalculated using current CHA and applicable equipment. Combat-generated aggro does not carry over.

DM CONTROL

DM-Dependent Decisions

  • Which instinct governs an encounter, especially when multiple species are present.
  • Which players match an enemy's instinct.
  • Whether an intelligent enemy can identify a rear-guard support player.
  • The CHA check DC and failure aggro for Intelligent Support Detection.
  • Whether a taunt works, its category, and the exact amount within its approved range.
  • Which player is targeted when multiple players have equal aggro.
  • When a new target selection is required.
  • Special enemy behaviour based on intelligence, species, personality, and encounter design.

OPEN EDGE CASES

Not Yet Specified

These are documented gaps, not permission to invent rules.

  1. AoE damage: Whether aggro is calculated separately for each enemy hit or once using total damage dealt.
  2. Mixed-INT encounters: How Low-INT size adjustments are applied across a mixed-intelligence encounter using one shared pool.
  3. Unconscious-player revival: Whether retained aggro becomes active immediately upon revival.
  4. Unconscious decay interaction: Whether -5 per unconscious round replaces or stacks with ordinary inactive-turn decay for the same interval.
  5. INT thresholds: Numerical boundaries for Low-, Average-, and High-INT classifications.
  6. Stealth decimals: Rounding convention for fractional aggro values.
  7. Target-selection timing: Mandatory frequency, if any, for re-spinning or selecting a new target.
  8. Direct item modifiers: Universal stacking order for multiple direct aggro-modifying items.
  9. Combined starting modifiers: Universal mathematical order for every combination of Stealth, size, and instinct adjustments.

CHANGE CONTROL

Established values and formulas must not be silently changed. DM-dependent rulings remain DM-dependent. Aggro System and Weapon Mastery remain separate unless an interaction is explicitly approved. Aggro System is priority 1 on the ideas list; Weapon Mastery remains the current development focus.
